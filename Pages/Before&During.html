Before & During a Flood

Flooding can happen after hours of heavy rainfall or when rivers overflow. Knowing how to prepare and respond can help protect you and your family.

Explore Safety Guidelines

01. Before a Flood

Get your household ready before the water starts rising.

Prepare Your Household

Families living near rivers or in low-lying areas should be prepared, especially during the rainy season. Do not wait for a flood warning to start making preparations.

  • Prepare an emergency bag with food, water and basic supplies.
  • Keep important documents in waterproof bags.
  • Check weather forecasts and flood warnings regularly.
  • Know the nearest evacuation centre and safe routes.
  • Charge your phone and prepare a flashlight.
  • Move valuable belongings and electrical appliances to higher places.
  • Discuss an emergency plan with your family.

02. Prepare an Emergency Bag

Pack the essentials you may need if you have to evacuate.

Food & Drinking Water

Bottled water, canned food, dry snacks and ready-to-eat meals. Choose food that can be stored without refrigeration.

First Aid & Hygiene

A first aid kit, essential medicines, hand sanitiser, soap, masks and other personal hygiene products.

Important Equipment

A flashlight, spare batteries, power bank, whistle, extra clothes and waterproof bags for important belongings.

03. During a Flood

Take action quickly when floodwater begins to rise.

Know When to Evacuate

During a flood, roads can become dangerous and water levels can rise quickly. Your safety is more important than saving your belongings.

  • Move to higher ground if floodwater approaches your home.
  • Follow evacuation instructions from local authorities.
  • Stay away from rivers and flooded roads.
  • Switch off electricity only if it is safe to reach the main switch.
  • Take your emergency bag when evacuating.
  • Listen to official announcements for updates.
  • Contact family members to let them know you are safe.

Help Others in Your Community

Flooding affects entire communities, not just individual households. Children, elderly people and people with disabilities may need extra help during evacuation.

  • Check whether neighbours need assistance.
  • Help others contact emergency services.
  • Share official flood warnings with your community.
  • Never put yourself in danger while helping others.

Do and Don't

Quick reminders.

Do

✓ Move to higher ground early
✓ Keep a go-bag ready
✓ Follow official warnings
✓ Help neighbours

Don't

✖ Walk through floodwater
✖ Touch electrical items when wet
✖ Wait until the last minute
✖ Drink untreated water
