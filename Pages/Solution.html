The Underground Discharge Tunnel

Discover how underground drainage can help manage floodwater.

What Is an Underground Discharge Tunnel?

An underground discharge tunnel is a large passage built beneath the ground to transport excess rainwater from one location to another.

It provides an additional drainage route when heavy rainfall produces more water than existing drainage systems can handle.

How Does It Work?

Rainwater enters through surface drains and underground inlet structures. It then flows through the tunnel towards a suitable outlet.

The system can use gravity or pumping equipment to move water, depending on the ground level and river conditions.

Tunnel Design and Structure

Our proposed cylindrical underground tunnel.

Cylindrical Tunnel Design

Our proposed tunnel has a cylindrical shape with a circular cross-section. This shape distributes surrounding ground pressure effectively when properly engineered.

Reinforced concrete lining helps provide structural strength and protects the tunnel from surrounding soil and groundwater.

Structural Support

The tunnel requires reinforced concrete lining and suitable ground support to withstand surrounding soil and water pressure.

Internal pillars are generally avoided because they obstruct water flow. Additional beams may support access shafts and other structures.

How Can It Reduce Flood Risk?

Potential benefits for communities in Taungoo.

01. Faster Water Transportation

Helps move excess rainwater away from flood-prone areas through an additional underground drainage route.

02. Less Surface Water

Reduces water accumulation on roads and nearby land when sufficient drainage capacity is available.

03. Improved Urban Drainage

Provides another pathway for managing stormwater during periods of heavy rainfall.

04. Reduced Drainage Pressure

Could reduce the amount of water entering existing drainage channels and help prevent overloading.

Challenges and Limitations

Important factors that must be considered.

High Construction Costs

Tunnels require expensive materials, machinery and skilled workers.

Soil Stability and Groundwater

Weak soil and groundwater pressure can make underground construction difficult.

Maintenance

Sediment and rubbish can accumulate inside the tunnel and reduce its capacity.

Environmental Impact

Construction and water discharge could affect surrounding land and ecosystems.

Discharge Location

A suitable outlet must be selected to avoid increasing flood risks downstream.
